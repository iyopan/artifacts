# Quotations behind Section V (and the leaderboard sentence of Section IV-B)

Each quotation is verbatim from the arXiv version named in the reference. "page" is the PDF page of that version on which a mechanical search located the quotation (line-break hyphenation joined). The proof-of-learning quotations ([64]-[66]) are from the arXiv abstracts and are listed at the end.

## [52] Eisenhofer et al., Verifiable and provably secure machine unlearning, SaTML 2025
Version read: arXiv:2210.09126v3 (5 Mar 2025)

- p. 2 (cited as p.~2): "the server computes a proof of training whenever data points are added to the model's training data: this establishes that it trained the model on a particular dataset"
- p. 7 (cited as p.~7): "must know some underlying dataset for these transcripts"
- p. 6 (cited as p.~6): "it is important to ensure that the server uses the most up-to-date model for inference,"
- p. 6 (cited as p.~6): "related to, but independent of the problem of unlearning"

## [53] Jia, Wyllie et al., Backdoor detection through replicated execution of outsourced training, SaTML 2025
Version read: arXiv:2504.00170v1 (31 Mar 2025)

- p. 3 (cited as p.~3): "access to n cloud providers, and not all n are malicious"
- p. 4 (cited as p.~4): "We assume the servers do not collude, and that the malicious servers do not use identical backdoors or hyperparameters"
- p. 13 (cited as p.~13): "assumes that a cloud provider that was initially benign does not later become adversarial"
- p. 11 (cited as Table~II, p.~11): "at least six benign servers"
- p. 12 (cited as p.~12): "if the number of malicious servers is greater than or equal to the number of benign servers, there could be many incorrect detections"
- p. 12 (cited as p.~12): "may want to submit the same training job multiple times (e.g., 5) to each server and to virtualize having more servers and run RTTD as if n=15"
- p. 12 (cited as p.~12): "may also lead to vulnerabilities that may be exploited by adaptive adversaries if they know the client would 'virtualize' the servers"

## [54] Zhang, Das, Kamath, Tramèr, Position: Membership inference attacks cannot prove that a model was trained on your data, SaTML 2025
Version read: arXiv:2409.19798v2 (7 Mar 2025)

- p. 1 (cited as p.~1): "cannot give reliable evidence of data usage in the training of production ML models, because it is impossible to bound the attack's false positive rate,"
- p. 1 (cited as p.~1): "we cannot sample from the null hypothesis"
- p. 3 (cited as p.~3): "originally samples the target data x randomly from some set X, before x is collected by the model developer"

- p. 8 (cited as p.~8): "carries no useful information, so that it does not impact other data published on the Web"

## [64] Jia et al., Proof-of-learning: definitions and practice, IEEE S&P 2021 (arXiv:2103.05633v1, abstract)

- "there is currently no mechanism for the entity which trained the model to prove that these parameters were indeed the result of this optimization procedure"
- "an adversary seeking to illegitimately manufacture a proof-of-learning needs to perform *at least* as much work than is needed for gradient descent itself" (quoted in the paper up to "as much work")

## [65] Zhang et al., "Adversarial examples" for proof-of-learning, IEEE S&P 2022 (arXiv:2108.09454v3, abstract)

- "in a similar way as optimizing an adversarial example, we could make an arbitrarily-chosen data point 'generate' a given model"
- "we are able to generate a valid proof with significantly less cost than generating a proof by the prover"

## [66] Fang et al., Proof-of-learning is currently more broken than you think, IEEE EuroS&P 2023 (arXiv:2208.03567v2, abstract)

- "a model owner logs training checkpoints to establish a proof of having expended the computation necessary for training"
- "while it is true that current PoL verification is not robust to adversaries, recent work has largely underestimated this lack of robustness"
- "one cannot develop a provably robust PoL verification mechanism without further understanding of optimization in deep learning"
